Do interim NFL head coaches actually do well?

football
NFL
head coaches
Author

Danny Berg

Published

December 5, 2025

A yearly occurrence in the National Football League, a handful of bottom-tier clubs will let their leader go in the middle of the season. Whether that is because they’re in the middle of a 14-game losing streak, are absolute morons, or might send an email or two, a few teams will look to shake up the locker room by firing the head coach.

Does it really do anything though? Are teams just trying to find a scapegoat and try and spiral out of control to a high draft pick? Or are things really just that desperate that anything could provide a spark? Continuity is always a puzzle, and disrupting it sometimes helps. Sometimes it doesn’t. It really depends.

But almost always, the firing of a head coach in the middle of the season means that the team has sucked. Bad.

The tight ends coach who was previously the head honcho gets put back into the spotlight. Jeff Saturday gets brought off his couch. That can really get the guys going. Usually. Recently speaking at least, the first game bump has not been super kind to the freshly wounded. And in those occasions where the interim coach gets his first win in his first game, that looks awful bad on the loser.

Over this sample size, interim coaches are winning games at just a 31.2% clip. Over the longer interim stints that these replacement coaches have, those results don’t show up. If the interim isn’t doing super hot, then the guy that was there beforehand didn’t do super well either (usually). The team is just not good. The offense might be good but the defense doesn’t exist. The defense might be electric but the offense can’t get a touchdown if their lives depended on it. Or maybe everything is bad! The point differentials usually are not going to look great.

On the left of the blue line represents the coach that began the season at the helm. The blue line itself represents their final game. A vast majority of the canned coaches were having a hard time staying at the break-even point. When the losses stack up, that point differential nosedives. Surprisingly, though, a lot of teams clean it up after moving out of an office.

As it turns out, firing a coach in-season really depends on a lot of factors. The structure of the organization, the players and supporting cast, and the overall support of the fan base play a big role in whether or not the interim can be successful in their new role. The remaining schedule can also play a big role as well, but the reality of “Any Given Sunday” shows up no matter what happens.
